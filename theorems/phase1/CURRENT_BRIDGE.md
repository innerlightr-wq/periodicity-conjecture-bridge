## What each numbered result actually says and needs

### Proposition 2.2 (the isometry)
`v₂(Φ(v) − Φ(v′)) = lcp(v, v′)` for all `v, v′ ∈ {0,1}ℕ`. Proved directly from the defining recursion `3^{k_n} x + c_n = 2^n T^n(x)`: `x mod 2^L` is determined by, and determines, the first `L` letters of `v(x)`. **This is a property of Φ alone** — no Sturmian input, no periodicity assumption, no balance assumption. It holds for the full shift `{0,1}ℕ`.

### Proposition 4.1 (rational value of a periodic word)
For a finite word `w` of length `ℓ` with `k ≥ 1` ones, `Φ(w^∞) = c_w/(2^ℓ − 3^k)`, where `c_w = Σ_{i<ℓ, w_i=1} 3^{k−k_{i+1}(w)} 2^i` is a specific positive integer built from `w`'s own one-positions. Proved by summing Proposition 2.1's series in geometric blocks. **Property of arbitrary periodic words** — `w` need not be mechanical, balanced, or anything else; the formula is purely combinatorial (unique factorization of `2^ℓ ≠ 3^k` is the only external fact used).

### Lemma 4.2 and Remark 4.3 (height control, and its failure)
Lemma 4.2: for `w = w_{p,q}` (the *mechanical* word of slope `p/q`), `0 < c_w ≤ q·max(2^q, 3^p)`. Proof uses the *exact* one-position count `k_{i+1}(w) ≥ (i+1)p/q` for mechanical words specifically. Remark 4.3 kills the naive generalization: for `w = 0^a1^a` (balanced as a *finite word*, but with **unbalanced periodization** `w^∞`), `c_w/(ℓ·max(2^ℓ,3^k)) = ((3/2)^a − 1)/2^a → ∞`, so **no constant `C` bounds `c_w ≤ Cℓ·max(2^ℓ,3^k)` over arbitrary words**. The remark also gives the correct generalization: for an arbitrary *balanced* `w` (discrepancy `< 1` in every prefix), the same proof goes through with an extra constant factor `3`. **This is exactly the height-control problem the present audit's Section 4 (`DISCREPANCY_HEIGHT_THEOREM.md`) must resolve at general discrepancy.**

### Theorem 5.3 / 8.3 (exact approximation-depth laws)
At slope `γ` (Theorem 8.3 is the general-slope version of Theorem 5.3, proved by the identical argument with `β,α` replaced by `γ,α_γ`), the depth `lcp(1c_γ, w_n^∞)` at the `n`-th continued-fraction convergent equals exactly `q_n − 1` (n even) or `q_n + q_{n+1} − 1` (n odd). This is **entirely continued-fraction machinery**: it uses the alternation of convergents, `α_γD_n < 1` (Lemma 8.2, itself just `q_{n+1} > α_γ`), and `p_n ≥ 2` for `n ≥ 3`. **No balance, no repetition exponent, no height bound enters this proof at all** — it is a statement about the combinatorics of `⌊jα_γ⌋` versus `⌊jα_n⌋`, i.e. about the *mechanical-word coding itself*, restricted to intercept 0.

### Theorems 6.1, 8.4, 8.5 (the Liouville comparison)
The integer `M_n = uδ_n − vc_n` (with `δ_n = 2^{q_n} − 3^{p_n}`) is shown nonzero via `v₂(M_n) = ` depth (finite by Theorem 5.3/8.3, hence `M_n ≠ 0`), giving `|M_n| ≥ 2^{depth}`. This is compared against the archimedean bound `|M_n| ≤ H(1+q_n)G_n` (Lemma 4.2 + `H = max(|u|,v)`), and the depth is shown to outrun the height for all `γ ∈ (0,1)`, with margin `c(γ) = 2 − max(1,θ)`, `θ = γ log₂3`, bounded below by `2 − log₂3 > 0` uniformly. **This is the abstract Liouville/integer-comparison step** — it needs only (a) a nonzero-integer lower bound from the 2-adic depth, and (b) an archimedean upper bound from a height estimate on `c_n`, `δ_n`. It is **entirely general and does not itself reference Sturmian combinatorics** — Sturmian-ness enters only through *which depth law and which height bound* are available to feed it.

### Proposition 9.2 / Corollary 9.3 (transported Dubickas counting bound)
Independent route. `Φ(s) ∈ Q ⟹ T^t(Φ(s)) = a_t/b` with `b` fixed and odd (Lemma 9.1, using **only** that `T` preserves an odd denominator — no growth-to-infinity assumption needed, unlike Dubickas's original positive-integer setting), and `|a_t|+b ≤ (3/2)^t(|a_0|+b)`. Distinct `a_t` in a window of size `2^{L-1}` are forced by aperiodicity + the isometry (Prop 2.2), giving `p_s(L) ≥ ⌊(L−1−log₂H₀)/λ⌋+1`, `λ = log₂(3/2)`. Corollary 9.3: `liminf_L p_s(L)/L < 1/λ = 1.70951...` forces `Φ(s) ∈ Q` to fail. **This is a pure complexity-counting argument — no periodic approximants, no depth law, no balance.** It is strictly weaker in general (only reaches `p_s(L) = o(1.70951L)`) but needs *nothing* beyond a complexity upper bound, which is why it is called "counting" throughout and contrasted with the "Liouville/repetition" route.

### Theorem 10.1 (the initial-square argument, all intercepts)
Replaces the depth law (ingredient (ii)) with: `s` begins in a square `WW` (Theorem 10.2, quoted), the root `W` of that square is a cyclic permutation of a standard word (Theorem 10.3, quoted) hence `W^∞` is *balanced*, so Lemma 10.4 (a corollary of Remark 4.3's balanced-word bound, constant `3`) gives `c_W ≤ 3ℓ·max(2^ℓ,3^k)`. Depth `≥ 2ℓ` (trivial, from the square itself) is compared against this height, giving margin `c(γ)ℓ` with the *same* `c(γ) = 2 − max(1,γlog₂3)` as Theorem 8.4. **This is the paper's own generalization step** — it already replaces "depth law from continued fractions" with "depth ≥ 2ℓ from an initial square," and already replaces "mechanical-word height bound" with "balanced-word height bound." The present audit's task is to push both replacements further: arbitrary `s` (not just Sturmian), arbitrary repetition exponent `r` (not just `r=2`), and arbitrary discrepancy `D` (not just bounded `D<1`).

### Theorem 10.2 (Allouche–Davison–Queffélec–Zamboni), Theorem 10.3 (Berthé–Holton–Zamboni), Lemma 10.4
Both quoted, both **Sturmian-specific**: 10.2 needs Sturmian-ness for "infinitely many initial squares"; 10.3 needs it for "primitive root of an initial `r≥2` power (`|w|≥2`) is a cyclic permutation of a standard word." The paper explicitly notes (line 1062-1067) that balance of `w` *alone* does **not** imply 10.3's conclusion (counterexamples: `0110, 1001, 01110, 10001` are balanced but not cyclic permutations of standard words) — **the paper is careful that "balanced" and "cyclic permutation of a standard word" are different hypotheses**, and Lemma 10.4 needs the latter (though its *proof* only uses balance of `W^∞`, obtained *via* Theorem 10.3). This distinction matters for the generalization: **the height bound (Lemma 10.4 / Remark 4.3's generalization) needs only balance of the periodized root, not the stronger "cyclic permutation of standard word" property** — so a class with balanced-but-not-standard-cyclic roots could still use the height bound, just not via quoting Theorem 10.3 verbatim.

### Remarks 9.5, 10.6, 10.7
9.5: the counting margin `2−log₂3` and the Liouville-route worst-case margin `c(γ)` coincide — "the two proofs trade repetition against height at the same rate," and **neither reaches complexity `2L`** (Rote words, rotation codings) — this is the paper's own explicit statement of exactly the frontier this audit targets. 10.6: the two Liouville sub-routes (continued-fraction depth law vs. initial-square depth) are independent, differing in which intercepts and which height floor they give. 10.7: warns against conflating the additive constant `q` in `T_q` with convergent denominators `q_n` — irrelevant to the generalization, but flags that a `qx+1`-style generalization of the *multiplier* would change the margin `c(γ)` to `2 − max(1,γlog₂q)`, vanishing at `γ = 2/log₂q` for `q ≥ 5` — **useful as a sanity check that the present paper's margin is not accidentally robust to all perturbations.**

### Section 12, Open Problem (4)
Verbatim: *"Complexity between the counting threshold and `2L`... Is `Φ(s) ∉ Q` for every non-eventually-periodic `s` with `p_s(L) ≤ 2L` for all large `L` — in particular for Rote words? The repetition method... needs, in place of Theorem 10.2 and Theorem 10.3, some substitute initial repetition and height bound valid on this wider class; none is known to the author. This is the natural next target for either method."* This is the exact problem this audit is commissioned to attack.

## Classification A–G

- **A. Properties of Φ alone**: Proposition 2.1 (series representation, converges for any `v` with infinitely many ones), Proposition 2.2 (isometry), Proposition 2.3 (conjugates the shift to `T`). All hold on the *full* shift `{0,1}ℕ`, no combinatorial hypothesis on `v` anywhere.
- **B. Properties of arbitrary periodic words**: Proposition 4.1 (`Φ(w^∞) = c_w/(2^ℓ−3^k)`) — combinatorial identity, holds for *every* finite `w`, no balance/mechanical assumption.
- **C. Sturmian-specific combinatorics**: Lemma 3.1/Proposition 3.2 (one-positions `⌊jα⌋` for the specific word `1c_β`); Theorem 10.2 (infinitely many initial squares — ADQZ 2001); Theorem 10.3 (initial-power roots are cyclic permutations of standard words — BHZ 2006); the mechanical-word one-position formula underlying Theorem 5.3/8.3's depth law.
- **D. Balance/discrepancy assumptions**: Lemma 4.2 (exact count for mechanical words, effectively discrepancy `0` up to floor/ceiling); Remark 4.3's generalization (discrepancy `<1`, constant `3`); Lemma 10.4 (discrepancy `<1` via Theorem 10.3, constant `3`, empirically `≤0.44` at `n≤40`).
- **E. Repetition assumptions**: Theorem 10.2's "arbitrarily long initial squares" (repetition exponent exactly `2`, used because `2 > max(1,γlog₂3)` for every Sturmian slope `γ∈(0,1)` — i.e. exponent `2` happens to always beat the height cost `max(1,γlog₂3) < log₂3 < 2`).
- **F. Continued-fraction assumptions**: Convention 5.1/8.1 and Lemma 5.2/Theorem 5.3/8.3's *entire* proof — alternation of convergents, `q_{n+1} > α_γ` (Lemma 8.2), the two exact convergent identities `p_{n+1}q_n − p_nq_{n+1} = (−1)^n` and `(p_n+p_{n+1})αD_n = 1+p_nα(D_n−D_{n+1})`. **Only used in the Section 5–8 route** (depth law at intercept 0), **not** in the Section 10 route (initial squares), which needs no continued-fraction identity at all — this is stated explicitly by the paper (line 1006-1007: "This section removes the restriction by a different route, which uses no depth law at all").
- **G. Assumptions needed only for quantitative height floors (not qualitative irrationality)**: the *exact* constant in Lemma 4.2/10.4 (whether `C=1` or `C=3` — the paper notes `C=1` appears empirically sufficient but is not proved); the precise convergent-based height floors of Corollary 6.2/8.7 (depend on the specific continued fraction of `γ`, hence qualitative irrationality of `Φ(s)` never needs them — only the *existence* of unboundedly many `q_n`, cf. Remark 6.3: "needs no irrationality measure for `β`, no bound on its partial quotients").

## What Section 10's proof structure isolates as the reusable template

Strip away the Sturmian-specific citations (Theorems 10.2, 10.3) and Theorem 10.1's proof reduces to exactly four steps, each already stated abstractly in the paper's own language:
1. `s` begins in an initial power `W^r` (`r ≥ 2` in the paper) with `W` primitive, `|W| = ℓ`.
2. `lcp(s, W^∞) ≥ rℓ` (trivial from the power).
3. A height bound `c_W ≤ F(W)` for some explicit `F` (Lemma 10.4 in the Sturmian case, `F(W) = 3ℓ·max(2^ℓ,3^k)`).
4. The comparison `|M| ≥ 2^{rℓ}` vs. `|M| ≤ H·(|δ|+c_W) ≤ H·(F(W) + max(2^ℓ,3^k))`, giving a margin that is positive once `rℓ − log₂F(W) → ∞`.

This is precisely the abstraction the task's Part 1 asks for, and it is confirmed here to be **already implicit in the paper's own Section 10 proof** — the present audit's job is to determine exactly how far step 3 (the height bound `F`) and step 1 (the repetition exponent `r`) can be relaxed before the comparison in step 4 stops closing.
