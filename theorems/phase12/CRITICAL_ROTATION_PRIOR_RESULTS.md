## 3. A downgrade required: T2 is specifically disputed

The repository records T2 throughout as "preprint". That is now too weak a caveat. An **open,
uncontested issue filed by a third party** (Tal Weiss, `innerlightr-wq/eoc-divergence` issue #30)
identifies the precise step carrying Theorem 1 and argues it does not follow:

> the paper passes from "`Φ_ℝ(v)` exists and is irrational" to "`Φ(v)` is aperiodic" via
> "the inverse limit `lim← Φ_ℝ(v)` is a 2-adic integer which is aperiodic, because the irrational
> `Φ_ℝ(v)` does not admit an eventually periodic 2-adic expansion." A real irrational has **no**
> 2-adic expansion; the real and 2-adic limits of one sequence of rationals are independent
> (`1+2+4+⋯` diverges in `ℝ` and equals `−1` in `ℤ₂`).

The objection does not claim T2 is false — it would follow from the Periodicity Conjecture — only that
its argument does not establish it. **Consequence for this repository's coverage map:** the securely
covered density region is `liminf < β` (T1, refereed), **not** `liminf ≠ β`. Density `> β` must be
listed as *not securely covered*. This does not change any verdict about Theorem 11 — our word is
uncovered under either reading — but it does change the shape of the residual region, and several
files state the stronger version. See `PHASE12_PROPOSED_UPDATES.md`.

## 4. Why the `p`-adic low-complexity theorems (T5) do not reach this

They bound the complexity of the **Hensel digit expansion** of a `p`-adic number. Our `s` is the
**parity vector** of `Φ(s)`, i.e. `s = Q_∞(Φ(s))`, not its digit string. `Φ` is a 2-adic isometry
(`v₂(Φ(a)−Φ(b)) = lcp(a,b)`) and conjugates the shift to `T`, **not** to multiplication by 2; it is
not digit-preserving, and no bound of either complexity by the other is known. Concretely: a
low-complexity *parity vector* may a priori have a high-complexity *digit string*, and T5 is about the
latter. In the other direction T5 gives nothing new either — if `Φ(s) ∈ ℚ` its digit string is
eventually periodic, which is exactly what T5's hypothesis assumes away.

## 5. Why the subspace-theorem route (T6) does not reach this either

Our approximants satisfy `L(ℓ(M)) ≥ ℓ(M) + M` with `ℓ(M) > 49M`, so `L/ℓ = 1 + O(M/ℓ) → 1`. A
Ridout/Schmidt argument needs an approximation exponent bounded away from `1`. The witnesses are
therefore far too weak for transcendence, which is why Theorem 11 excludes only **rationality**. This
matches the foundation paper's own open problem (3), which asks for transcendence and is untouched
here.

## 6. Where Theorem 11 sits relative to the foundation paper's own open problems

The foundation paper (`reference/sturmian-conjugacy-map-irrational-2adic-integer.pdf`) asks, verbatim:

> **(4) Complexity between the counting threshold and `2L`.** "… Is `Φ(s) ∉ ℚ` for every
> non-eventually-periodic `s` with `p_s(L) ≤ 2L` for all large `L` — in particular for Rote words?
> The repetition method of Sections 6–10 needs, in place of Theorem 10.2 and Theorem 10.3, some
> substitute initial repetition and height bound valid on this wider class; **none is known to the
> author.** This is the natural next target for either method."

Theorem 11 supplies such a substitute for one explicit word, and the general theorem of
`QUADRATIC_RATIONAL_INTERCEPT_THEOREM.md` supplies it for a two-parameter family. It does **not**
answer problem (4), which quantifies over all `p_s(L) ≤ 2L`.

**Attribution point that must not be lost.** The paper's problem (1) already proposes the mechanism:

> "The natural replacement is the **first entry time of the orbit `{jγ+ρ}` into an interval of length
> `jD_n` at the partition endpoint**, which the three-distance theorem and the Ostrowski expansion of
> `ρ` compute."

That is Theorem 11's R1 identity. **The hitting-time idea is the foundation paper's own, not new in
Phase 11.** What Phase 11 added is its execution at complexity `2n` (two discontinuities instead of
one), the split of the two arcs into an elementary bound at `0` and a Baker bound at `β`, and the
witness-size calculus.
