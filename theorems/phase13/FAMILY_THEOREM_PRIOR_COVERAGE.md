> `Φ(s) ∉ ℚ` for `s_n = χ_{[0,β)}({nα+ρ})`, `α` quadratic irrational, `ρ` real algebraic,

## 1. Standing rule on López–Stoll, applied throughout

**T2 = López & Stoll, *The 3x+1 periodicity conjecture in ℝ*, arXiv:2101.12747 (2021), Theorem 1**
("a divergent trajectory of `ζ ∈ ℚ_odd` has `liminf h/ℓ = ln2/ln3`") is treated here as a
**challenged preprint claim pending verification — neither disproved nor securely established — and
is not relied on anywhere.** The challenge is `innerlightr-wq/eoc-divergence` issue #30 (open,
uncontested), which identifies the step from "`Φ_ℝ(v)` is irrational" to "`Φ(v)` is aperiodic" —
routed through an alleged 2-adic expansion of a real irrational — and argues it does not follow. The
foundation paper independently flags the same result as "whose proof is archimedean and whose source
is an unpublished preprint".

**Consequence for the coverage map, stated once:** the region securely covered by density is
`liminf < β` only. The residual region is `liminf ≥ β`, of which our family occupies `= β`. Nothing
below uses T2 in either direction.

## 3. Why the family's parameters place it outside T1–T4 by hypothesis, not by search

This is the part that is a proof rather than a literature note. Ones-density is exactly `β` (Weyl;
the limit exists), so the density criteria T1/T2 are inapplicable **by their own strict
inequalities**. Complexity is exactly `2n` (T13, with `β ∉ ℤα+ℤ` automatic because `β` is
transcendental and `α` algebraic), so `liminf p(L)/L = 2 > 1.70951…` and T3 is inapplicable. `s` is
not Sturmian, so T4 is inapplicable. None of these four depends on a search having been exhaustive.

## 4. The `p`-adic low-complexity theorems (T5) — the object is different

They bound the complexity of the **Hensel digit expansion**. Our `s` is the **parity vector**:
`s = Q_∞(Φ(s))`, where `Q_∞` is the Bernstein–Lagarias coding of the `T`-orbit. `Φ` is a 2-adic
isometry and conjugates the shift to `T`, **not to multiplication by 2**; it is not digit-preserving,
and no bound of either complexity by the other is known.

The distinction is worth stating with the two numbers named. Let `D(s) := Σ_i s_i 2^i` be the 2-adic
integer whose **digits** are `s`. Then:

- `D(s)` **is** covered by the T5 line whenever `s` has low complexity — and for `s` Sturmian,
  Bugeaud–Kekeç place such numbers in Mahler's `S`-, `T`- or `U₁`-classes, hence transcendental.
- `Φ(s) ≠ D(s)`, and our theorem is about `Φ(s)`. Nothing transfers.

In the other direction T5 gives nothing either: if `Φ(s) ∈ ℚ` its digit string is eventually
periodic, which is what T5's hypothesis assumes away.

## 5. Ridout's threshold — Phase 12's dismissal is withdrawn

Phase 12 dismissed the subspace-theorem route in one line: "our witnesses give `L/ℓ → 1`, far too
weak". **That was measured on the constructed family only, and it is misleading.** Bugeaud–Kekeç's
Theorem 1.3 (Ridout 1958) needs, for infinitely many `n`,

```
0 < | xi - x_n/y_n |_p  <  |x_n, y_n|^{-2-eps} ,
```

i.e. in our notation `L(ℓ) > (2+ε)·log₂ H_red(ℓ)` where `H_red` is the height of the **reduced**
`c_W/δ_W`. Since `log₂H_red ≤ log₂F(W) = ℓ + O(log ℓ)`, the threshold is essentially `L/ℓ > 2`.
Measured over every convergent denominator `3 ≤ q ≤ 28657` of the named word
(`scripts/phase13/ridout_exponent.py`):

```
ell     L        log2 H_red   E = L/log2 H_red   > 2 ?
8       24       9.12         2.631              yes
55      147      59.46        2.472              yes
377     846      383.66       2.205              yes
610     1456     617.26       2.359              yes
987     2443     995.13       2.455              yes
4181    9243     4190.94      2.205              yes
6765    22773    6774.98      3.361              yes
...                                              (7 of 20 convergents exceed 2)
```

So the exponent **exceeds Ridout's threshold at 7 of the 20 convergents tested, with a maximum of
`3.361`** — it is not "far too weak"; it is *sometimes comfortably past the threshold*. What is
missing is infinitude with a fixed `ε`: the criterion needs `E > 2+ε` for infinitely many `n`, which
is exactly the statement

> **`m*(ℓ)/ℓ > 1 + ε` for infinitely many convergent denominators `ℓ`,**

`m*` being the hitting time of §7 of the theorem file. Measured distribution of `m*(ℓ)/ℓ` over those
20 convergents: min `0`, median `0.904`, max `2.366`, exceeding `1` in `8` of `20`. **This is an
open combinatorial question about hitting times, and nothing here claims an answer.** 7-of-20 with no
visible pattern is not evidence of infinitude; the construction of the theorem deliberately drives
`E → 1`, so it is no evidence either way.

**If** the statement held, Ridout would give **transcendence** of `Φ(s)`, answering the foundation
paper's §12 open problem (3) for this word — a strictly stronger conclusion than Theorem 13. That
makes it the most valuable open question this audit surfaced, and it is recorded as such in
`PHASE13_VERDICT.md` §8. **It is not claimed, and no partial result toward it is claimed.**

## 6. The Hecke–Mahler line (T8, T9) — a different series, and archimedean

Hecke–Mahler series are `S_ρ(λ) = Σ_{k≥1}⌊kρ⌋λ^k` and their two-variable relatives, evaluated at
algebraic `λ` with `0 < |λ| < 1`. Two obstructions, each independently fatal:

1. **Archimedean.** T8/T9 are statements about complex/real values. Our value lives in `ℤ₂` and its
   smallness is 2-adic. The foundation paper's own problem (3) says precisely this: "The archimedean
   analogue is known for the Hecke–Mahler values strictly inside the domain [5]; here one would want
   a **2-adic Mahler method** along the continued-fraction chain." By the author's own assessment the
   2-adic side is open.
2. **The exponent sequence is not a Beatty sequence.** Hecke–Mahler coefficients are `⌊kρ⌋` — a
   Beatty sequence, which arises when the coding interval length **equals** the rotation number. Our
   `Φ(s) = −Σ_i s_i 2^i 3^{−k_{i+1}}` has `k_N = #\{n<N : \{nα+ρ\} ∈ [0,β)\}`, a Birkhoff sum of an
   indicator over an interval of length `β ≠ α`. That is **not** `⌊Nβ+c⌋` for any `c`: it is a
   generalised-Beatty / Rote count, with a three-distance structure but no floor-function closed
   form. So even the archimedean analogue of our object is not a Hecke–Mahler value.

The critical-density coincidence `2·3^{−β} = 1` — which makes the terms of `Φ(s)` of constant
archimedean size — is exactly why the archimedean series diverges at `γ = β` (foundation paper's own
remark) and why only the 2-adic place is available. §11 of the foundation paper is titled "Why the
2-adic place".
