F3  The double-failure construction, and what it does and does not establish.
    To defeat BOTH branches simultaneously the slope/intercept must satisfy three things:
      (i)   a_k EVEN for all large k with both boundary numerator parities odd, so that
            EVERY convergent numerator p_k is odd  -> the x-root always has odd weight;
      (ii)  d_k EVEN for all large k, so that the y-root weight d_k p_(k-1) + p_(k-2)
            == d_k + 1 == 1 (mod 2) is odd too  -> NO guaranteed even-weight root at all;
      (iii) tower-fast growth of a_k, so that the estimated discrepancy penalty outruns
            the driver q_(k-2)(m_(k-1)-1) and the growth condition (H) breaks.
    Note (ii) rules out the keep-one intercept: there d_k = 1 is ODD, so on family (i)
    the y-root weight p_(k-1) + p_(k-2) is EVEN and the even branch works.  The hostile
    intercept must therefore be an even-d_k one, e.g. c_k = a_k - 2.

    a  = [3, 1, 2*2^(2^1), 2*2^(2^2), 2*2^(2^3), ...]  (all even from k=3)
    c  = a_k - 2  (so d_k = 2, EVEN for every k)   admissible: True
    all convergent numerators odd: True
    guaranteed roots at scale k: x-root weight p_k (ODD), y-root weight
      2 p_(k-1) + p_(k-2) == 0 + 1 == 1 (ODD).  So NO guaranteed even-weight root exists
      at ANY scale -- this is a structural statement, not a sampling artefact.

    The first two hostile scales have ell_k = 93 and 2870, which ARE buildable, and (H)
    already fails at both (ratios 0.357 and 0.957).  So this is a direct test of whether
    (b) failing implies (c) failing.

    not a counterexample to Phi(v) not in Q, and not a counterexample to the bridge.
